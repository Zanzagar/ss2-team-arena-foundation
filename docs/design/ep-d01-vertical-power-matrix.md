# EP-D01 option matrix — what should keep growing?

**Status:** non-authoritative owner-discussion matrix. It does not approve

implementation. A decision becomes durable only through exact replay and the
[decision record](endless-progression-decisions.md).

## The actual decision

EP-D01 is larger than “should level stop at 50?” The displayed career counter
can continue independently of combat power. The decision is which kinds of
advantage continued play may add to one legal combat build:

| Kind of progression | What grows | Is it vertical power? | Why the distinction matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active scalar power | Damage, health, armour, accuracy/defence budget, or ordinary item-chassis budget in the build entering combat | Yes | If unbounded, enough time eventually overcomes encounter design and makes earlier gear/content obsolete. |
| Active rule capacity | How many behavior-changing effects can operate together | Yes | Even with capped stats, more simultaneous rules can create universal packages, loops, permanent control, or invulnerability. EP-D02 owns the proposed four-Load limit. |
| Portfolio breadth | How many different legal builds the player owns but cannot activate together | Not automatically | This is enjoyable growth only while loadout limits and commitment windows force the player to leave desirable answers behind. |
| Encounter access | Which rivals, doctrines, routes, Contracts, and difficulty tiers may be attempted | No | Harder or stranger problems can continue indefinitely without making the player's old numbers false. |
| Knowledge and execution | What the player understands and how well a team coordinates | No | This is mastery progression: the player improves even when the save's combat budget does not. |
| Prestige and convenience | Records, titles, cosmetics, history, presets, or reduced menu friction | No combat power | These can mark an endless career honestly, but cannot carry the whole loop for a player motivated mainly by strength. |

The key philosophical distinction is between **active power** and **portfolio
power**. A capped gladiator who owns twenty compelling effects is more flexible
than one who owns four, even if both may activate only four Load. That breadth
is meaningful progression. It becomes game-breaking if exact opponent
knowledge plus free swapping lets the larger library answer every fight without
sacrificing anything.

## What makes opportunity cost enjoyable

Opportunity cost is not automatically fun merely because a cap exists. It is
most likely to feel good when all four conditions hold:

1. **Desirable alternatives:** the player gives up another plan they genuinely
   wanted, rather than discarding obvious trash.
2. **Legible consequences:** the choice changes actions, sequencing, targets,
   or resource use in a way the player can predict.
3. **A meaningful commitment window:** the choice lasts long enough to test the
   plan, but ends at a known boundary where correction is possible.
4. **Loss without entrapment:** choosing A closes B for this Circuit or build,
   not necessarily for the entire save.

The current proposal's “own broadly, equip narrowly, lock for one Circuit, one
bounded Pivot” shape aims at this middle. Free per-fight respec erases the cost;
permanent blind branching turns experimentation into irreversible regret.

## Progression consequences by system

The current recommendation is not “progression stops.” It chooses where the
progression burden moves:

| System | Under A, the recommended fixed ceiling | If raw vertical growth continues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Career level and XP | Continue as history, system cadence, lateral choices, authored encounters, and eventually honest records/cosmetics. An empty mechanical catalog must stop pretending an XP ding is a power choice. | Every level needs a durable power delta or becomes a fake promise; the XP curve and catch-up economy become permanent balance infrastructure. |
| Loot | Chassis reaches a lasting ceiling; later rarity changes behavior, constraints, synergies, and liabilities. Older capped items can remain build-relevant. | Higher origin tiers replace earlier gear. Drop frequency, salvage, Forge costs, and enemy rewards must inflate forever without creating one dominant farm. |
| Opponents | Challenge comes from composition, doctrine, phases, route locks, team problems, and disclosed liabilities inside a bounded power envelope. | Opponent raw budgets must rise or normalize. If they read player power, upgrades feel cancelled; if they do not, one side eventually outscales the other. |
| Opportunity cost | Moves from “can I afford the higher number?” to “which desirable behaviors fit this active budget for this commitment window?” | Often collapses to “take the largest affordable number,” unless a second lateral budget is layered on top. |
| Co-op | Veteran breadth can be clamped by active budgets, making knowledge and preparation helpful without letting age delete a newer ally's choices. | Power gaps encourage carrying, reward funneling, exclusion, or aggressive normalization. |
| Economy | Needs bounded useful sinks, collection goals, experimentation, and cosmetics after chassis completion. | Inflation remains useful but requires perpetual price/reward scaling and overflow-safe persistence. |
| Content authoring | A finite stat envelope makes bosses and counterplay testable; ongoing work expands the grammar rather than every numeric range. | Every new tier reopens all time-to-kill, reward, AI, and item assumptions. Procedural scaling is cheap to generate but expensive to keep interesting. |

## Cap placement and opponent symmetry are separate subdecisions

Choosing a fixed ceiling does not automatically prove that the ceiling should
be exactly 50 or define whether bosses may exceed it.

### Where to place it

| Choice | Advantage | Disadvantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Designed tier 50 as a product promise** — current proposal | Legible; matches the proposed system timeline and community-reconstructed ordinary equipment/boss endpoint near 48–50. | The exact vertical budget is still unauthored, and the fingerprinted build's complete opponent curve is unverified. The familiar number can masquerade as measured truth. |
| **Accept the hard-ceiling philosophy now; tune the exact tier later** | Separates the durable design principle from a balance parameter and permits playtesting 40/50/60. | Leaves milestone pacing, UI promises, content cadence, and migrations provisional longer. |
| **Define the cap by authored content rather than career number** | Power stops when every intended vertical budget band has been mastered; avoids worshipping an arbitrary number. | Harder to communicate, and content additions can silently move what looked like a permanent endpoint. |

### Who obeys it

| Choice | Advantage | Disadvantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Symmetric envelope** — standard players and opponents share the bounded raw-power grammar | Difficulty must come from readable behavior, composition, and execution; players can trust that grinding is not the hidden answer. | Authoring genuinely harder late encounters is more demanding. Poor lateral design makes challenge feel samey. |
| **Bounded authored boss exceptions** | A rare oversized opponent can create spectacle and make survival/resource plans matter. | An exception can become a disguised stat check. It needs a disclosed liability and a strict bound or it erodes the ceiling immediately. |
| **Unbounded enemy growth against capped players** | Supplies endless threat without giving players infinite numbers. | Mathematically terminates in unwinnable attrition or forces hidden normalization. It is not a stable endless contract. |

## Current recommendation: A with explicit portfolio rules

The best fit for the stated goal is an early, satisfying vertical climb followed
by a fixed **active** ceiling and continuing **portfolio** progression:

1. Preserve the familiar Swords & Sandals fantasy of becoming visibly stronger
   through the authored early career.
2. At the ceiling, freeze raw stat, ordinary chassis, and simultaneous rule
   capacity. Continued levels unlock problems, alternatives, identities,
   collection progress, and records—not another disguised scalar multiplier.
3. Let ownership breadth grow, because discovery is progression. Keep active
   builds narrow and committed for a Circuit so owning every answer never means
   using every answer at once.
4. Allow correction between Circuits and a tightly bounded in-Circuit Pivot.
   The cost is giving up another attractive plan for a meaningful stretch, not
   permanent regret over an uninformed early click.
5. Keep standard opponent raw power inside the same envelope. Produce late
   difficulty through authored doctrines, combinations, liabilities, resource
   pressure, and coordination.
6. Treat tier 50 as a deliberate product target, not a vanilla measurement.
   The exact budget and whether 50 survives playtesting remain explicit gates.

This hybrid keeps the two strongest pleasures instead of choosing only one:
the first half delivers accumulation and equipment escalation; the long game
delivers mastery, collection, preparation, and expressive opportunity cost.
Its central risk is severe but visible: the lateral library must contain real,
non-dominated alternatives, and the commitment rules must stop a large
collection from becoming a universal counter kit.

## Owner discussion frontier

These questions select a direction; none closes EP-D01 yet.

1. **Late-career fantasy:** after the vertical climb, which satisfactions should
   dominate—becoming numerically stronger, becoming more strategically
   expressive, collecting more possible builds, conquering new encounter
   grammar, or accumulating prestige/history? A ranked answer is more useful
   than choosing only one.
2. **Age advantage:** against the same encounter with the same active build and
   equally skilled play, should a level-137 gladiator beat a level-50 gladiator
   because of career age alone: never, by a small persistent edge, or
   decisively?
3. **Commitment:** should players own broadly but equip narrowly for a Circuit,
   make permanent mutually exclusive career branches, or respec freely after
   seeing each fight? The recommendation is Circuit commitment with bounded
   correction.
4. **Opponent symmetry:** should standard late opponents share the player's raw
   ceiling, may rare bosses receive bounded disclosed exceptions, or should
   enemy numbers continue indefinitely?
5. **The number 50:** is tier 50 itself an important promise, or should we first
   accept the fixed-ceiling philosophy and select the exact cap only after
   authored budgets and playtests exist?
